My name is Nikhil Rajan. I recently completed my undergraduate degree in Mathematical Economics at the Madras School of Economics and am now pursuing my postgraduate degree in quantitative finance at the London School of Economics. What drew me in was seeing how ideas from measure theory, especially risk-neutral expectations, are used directly in option pricing models like Black-Scholes.

Since then, I have been building and testing models around that: simulating GBM paths, pricing options with Monte Carlo, and checking how sensitive outputs are to discretisation, volatility, and parameter shifts. Most of this I have implemented in Python and C++, with particular interest in how pricing errors, convergence, and simulation variance behave under different modelling choices.

I am most interested in how model outputs change when you vary things like the time step, volatility assumptions, or the numerical scheme being used. My current projects span market microstructure (liquidity regimes as Markov chains), credit risk (reconstructing the Jarrow-Lando-Turnbull framework from first principles, and a hidden Markov model for credit stress detection), financial contagion between the S&P 500 and Nifty 50, and a dynamic welfare analysis of sin taxation using Markov consumption models calibrated to NSSO data.

At Madras School of Economics, I have held seven teaching assistantships covering stochastic processes, game theory, time series analysis, microeconomics, multivariable calculus, and finance. I maintained a CGPA of 9.61 and was awarded the University Gold Medal for graduating Rank 1 in my batch. I have also been on the Dean's List in every graded semester.

Outside academics, I am a huge football fan, especially of Real Madrid CF, and closely follow both La Liga and the Premier League. For fun, I sometimes build small prediction models around football using recent form, player ratings, xG trends, injuries, and fixture difficulty to estimate title races and relegation probabilities over a season. I also follow cricket across all formats and end up watching almost every major sporting event at some point.

London School of Economics and Political Science
MSc Financial Statistics
London, United Kingdom · 2026 to 2027
ST409Stochastic Calculus
Brownian motion and its properties; construction and properties of the Itô stochastic integral with respect to Brownian motion, continuous local martingales, and Itô processes; Itô’s formula for functions of Brownian motion and Itô processes; exponential martingales and Lévy’s characterisation of Brownian motion; stochastic differential equations, including weak and strong solutions and conditions for existence and uniqueness; martingale representation theorem; Girsanov’s theorem and changes of probability measure and drift; and the connection between stochastic processes and partial differential equations through the Feynman-Kac theorem, with applications to continuous-time financial models.
ST461Mathematics of Market Microstructure
The mathematical foundations of algorithmic and high-frequency trading. Covers optimal execution (Almgren-Chriss, Obizhaeva-Wang) and market making with inventory and price-impact models (Avellaneda-Stoikov), built on stochastic control and Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations. Extends to market equilibrium under asymmetric information, covering the Kyle and Glosten-Milgrom models and limit order book dynamics, using stochastic filtering (Kalman). Concludes with modern market structure: decentralised exchanges, automated market making, and dark pools.
MA415The Mathematics of the Black and Scholes Theory
How option pricing really works. It begins with the binomial tree and replication, then moves into continuous time for the Black-Scholes PDE and the formulas for calls and puts. After that it's hedging with PDEs, implied and stochastic volatility, and pricing FX options. The maths gets heavy once the PDEs start.
ST463Stochastic Simulation, Training and Calibration
Simulation and machine learning put to work on risk. You do Monte Carlo and simulate stochastic processes, then move from simple linear models all the way up to deep neural networks and GANs. The hard part is getting these models calibrated properly. It finishes with reinforcement learning for hedging and pricing swing options in energy markets.
FM413Fixed Income Markets
Pretty much everything to do with bonds and interest rate products. It starts with yield, duration and convexity, then curve fitting, bootstrapping and hedging. You cover government and corporate bonds, including the convertible, callable and puttable kinds, plus caps, floors, swaps and swaptions. Mortgage-backed securities and credit risk transfer come in near the end.
ST436Financial Statistics I
The hands-on stats course, all done in R. You work with real financial data, both low and high frequency, fit ARCH type models for volatility, and go through Markowitz portfolio theory and the CAPM. There's also machine learning for forecasting, Value at Risk and a few simple trading strategies.
ST458Financial Statistics II
The second half of the stats sequence, and it leans a lot more into machine learning. You cover decision trees, random forests and gradient boosting, then neural networks and LSTMs on financial data. It also gets into factor models, cointegration, Granger causality, and building portfolios in high frequency data.
ST460Statistics for Finance
Asymptotic theory and the foundations of statistical inference. Convergence in probability and distribution, the law of large numbers and central limit theorem, Slutsky’s theorem and the delta method; sufficiency, exponential families and the Rao-Blackwell theorem; maximum likelihood, Fisher information and the Cramér-Rao lower bound, with consistency, asymptotic normality and efficiency; hypothesis testing through the Neyman-Pearson lemma, likelihood-ratio, Wald and score tests, and Wilks’ theorem; control of the false discovery rate under multiplicity; Bayesian inference and decision theory; and the Gauss-Markov theorem, robust inference, and the distinction between association and causation in linear models.
